Sustained participation and quality of life after three decades of phrenic nerve stimulation and home mechanical ventilation in high cervical spinal cord injury

Laura Peiler, Nabil Ktari, Maria-Cristina Hallwachs, Sven Hirschfeld, Martin Groß
Springer Nature · Spinal Cord · Case Report (Accepted 2026)

Abstract

Healthcare professionals generally underestimate quality of life (QOL) after high cervical spinal cord injury (SCI) with ventilator dependence compared with patient self-reports. This case provides long-term observational data from a community-dwelling individual with high cervical spinal cord injury...

Conclusion

Complete high cervical tetraplegia with ventilator dependence was compatible with three decades of community living, professional leadership, and reported well-being. Factors associated with this outcome included community-based living, family resources, the ability to speak, and sustained engagement in meaningful activity.
